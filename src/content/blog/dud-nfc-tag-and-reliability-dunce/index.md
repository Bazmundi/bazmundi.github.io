I have been looking for tags to 1) manage my filament spools and 2) look at what I need do to better manage all the food we stash in the freezer.



So I got 10 FIT0313 tags from an [AUS supplier](https://www.google.com/search?q=FIT0313+site%3A.au&rlz=1C1CHBF_en-GBAU863AU863&oq=FIT0313+site%3A.au&aqs=chrome..69i57.6819j0j4&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8).



I downloaded NXF Reader and NXF Writer onto my Android phone (Samsung A8).



I found 1 of 9 tags that would not write?!



The supplier claimed he had seen 1k tags with no errors, presumably a clumsy claim that he had not supplied me with a dud and so I was not entitled to a reimbursement OR the guy flat out had no idea about reliability. Its hard to tell the difference.



It is is clumsy argument since any one device could, in fact, be dud off the factory floor. QC departments only do so much testing. In fact, the warranty on devices is actually a trade-off manufacturers make against 100% testing.



So, go figure, the HP Spectre laptop I am typing this on is the second one I picked up of Office Works on the same day. I got the first one home, found a problem with its configuration and a broken key to boot. Straight out of the box by the way. So, took it back and got it swapped out on what is termed DOA - Dead On Arrival.



So, the idea that, even if the guy was telling the truth and had actually programmed 1k tags with no fails, reliability engineering would pessimistically tell you the tags had a 50:50 chance of a 1/1000 failure the next tag you used.



Now, it turns out [NXF Tag Info](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nxp.taginfolite) and [NXF Tag Writer](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nxp.nfc.tagwriter) were fine with reading all the tags but one but would not write to any of them (likely since NXF apparently only spoke to certain "compatible" chips) and that same one would not reliably read each time.



So I downloaded [NFC Tools](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wakdev.wdnfc) and found I could write to 9 of 10 tags and that same old pesky tag, the seemingly dud one that the NXF apps baulked at, came up as unable to write in NFC Tools.



So, 9 of 10 tags from China worked and one did not. Not a real surprise. I have thrown out stacks of junk electronics from China, purchased via Aliexpress, that was dud. You might recall my run-in with OrangePi Zero? Though that was a dud expansion board that then fragged a main board.



So, with 9 of 10 tags, I went for broke and went premium on [RollingUp](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.printing3d.spoolsmanager) so I can now try to keep better track of my filament reels.



All I need now is sort out a means to set up to tag freezer containers with contents.
