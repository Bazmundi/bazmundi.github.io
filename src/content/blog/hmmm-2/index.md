Finally put my Finder back together. I was getting separation layers and the finder help team told me I needed to replace the flat cable to the extruder. Assuming that it might be the connector on the board, I also replaced that. So a semi-difficult disassembly then re-assembly. But everything buzzed out okay.



Except the hot-end seemed to be clogged??!?!



Well, it would have been, sitting there for so long. But it was quite cantankerous and I could not get the thing unclogged.



In a huff, I have ordered a couple of packets of 0.4mm stainless wire widgets for unclogging - I should have had those from the start.



But, as I had the thing apart, I had a look at the extruder and it seemed that the serrated drive pinion was actually too close to the motor and so the teeth were not fully biting into the filament. In fact, the teeth were a surrounded either side by a flat. So it seemed the feeder may actually be slipping.



So I pulled the extruder apart and adjusted the toothed drive pinion.



When I put it back together and ran a pre-feed, there was markedly more oomph in the feed. It certainly unclogged itself, pushing out a load of crap for a while before the fine filament arrived.



Running now a couple of prints of feeders for the manual pick and place and all is again working fine.



As well as the stainless steel wire for unclogging, I ordered a packet of hot end cleaning filament. So, I will aim to keep better care of the hot end from now on.



Whether the new flat cable and board fixed the original problem, or whether it was the misaligned serrated drive pinion which was dropping of layers, I'll never know. Whether I see or not the problem is fixed, I will not see until I try a larger print. What fun it it turns out it was the slipping filament feed, because of the pinion alignment, and not the cabling.
