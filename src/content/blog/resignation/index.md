... I'll need DIY it.



The makerspace I attend occasionally has an interesting way about it.



I did the obligatory welding induction, bought some scrap, plumb forgot the welder setup, so thought I might do the induction again and jump straight into consolidation post second course. But of course, the chap that runs the inductions tells me he is no longer going to run the inductions as he's too busy. Whilst, as it was, he was in the midst of using the welding setup at the makerspace as his own little project space.



I did the obligatory induction on the CNC machine. It wasn't a hands on course, you had to spend through the nose for that. So, I gotz me an Easel account and modeled up a holder for my Watcom digitiser.



However, I suspect the instructor hadn't thought that through as when I went to use the CNC setup on me own. It turns out if you're wanting to use your personal Easle account on the makerspace CNC machine then Easle wants, essentially, to install a user account locked driver for the CNC machine. So, a bit hard to do that without admin rights to the makerspace laptop. Didn't help Easle on the makerspace machine wanted the instructors login.



Really didn't help the CNC course instructor was on holiday. Really, REALLY didn't help no one else, manager included, had any clue about the CNC machine and its setup. That was getting towards a deal breaker as you would think they would train the volunteers on the equipment and so at least have rudimentary knowledge to triage an account login.



You would have thought the manager at least would have login details for all the accounts running on computers inside the makerspace he was managing. Even better, you'd think the makerspace would have a login, and not rely upon what appeared to be an account owned by a volunteer.



Is the guy a volunteer or just someone interested in CNC and so they let him act as a pseudo volunteer?



Outside of that, they won't give out the details of that one and only one person who knows the CNC machine, can't tell you when he's in, and having tried half a dozen times turning up on the off chance the guy who understands the CNC is there has been a FRACKING drag, to say the least. In fact the CNC induction runs so infrequently to suggest the makerspace hasn't got real support for the CNC machine.



What bugs me more, its been months and I'd say no one working at the makerspace actually proactively looked at how to sort that issue with logging into the CNC setup, even down to something simple like updating the notes stuffed in the drawer of the CNC with the login details for the machine.



So, roughing out the price of doing the hands on course (over AUS$250 from memory), and the $20 a pop space fee, you could spend well and truely more than what I estimated was the likely cost to build the famous Dremel CNC machine of [Nikus](https://www.instructables.com/member/Nikus/).



Hence, I got 2 Kg of PLA+ instead of PETG and well on me way to building one for home to use with me Dremel. If I want to do a job in excess of its capability, I'll think about going to the makerspace issues by setting up a gcode sender on me laptop and circumventing the lack of facilitation at the makerspace for CNC savvy.



But first I have to learn a bit more about CNCing.



Outside of that, I am putting together the bits for a PnP for the Hackerspace group I hang with (electronics hackers and nerds). A simple setup for small smd jobs and learning OpenPnP etc.
